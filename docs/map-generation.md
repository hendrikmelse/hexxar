# Map generation

`packages/shared/src/generate.ts` builds boards in five flavors (the fair ones for two, three, four and six players, and random ones for any number). `createDuelMatch` makes the fair two-player boards (games use it for duels); `createTrioMatch`, `createQuadMatch` and `createSextetMatch` make the fair boards for three, four and six players (nothing in a room uses them yet); `createFreeForAllMatch` makes random boards for any number of players (battle royales). Given the same options and seed, both always produce the same board. Cities, villages and farmland follow the same rules in both (see Tile types), and the code for placing them is shared. Both draw their land the same way, from a **blueprint** of round chambers ("blobs") joined by corridors, which `royale/` and `duel/` lay out and rasterize onto hexes.

## Duel boards

A duel board is **exactly symmetric**, in one of two ways, picked by the seed (or by the `symmetry` option): a **mirror**, or a **half turn** (180 degrees about the middle). A mirror board is mirrored across one of two lines, picked by the seed (half each, or by the `axis` option): a vertical line through the middle of the board, which runs through tile corners (the line goes through the "points" of the center tile), or a line turned 30 degrees from that, which runs through the middles of tile edges. Both are exact on hexes: the second is a flip followed by a 120 degree turn. After a mirror board is made, the whole board is **turned by a random multiple of 60 degrees** (six choices, one of them no turn), so the line can lie any of the six ways a hex mirror line can: vertical, or one of the two diagonals for the first kind, and sideways or one of the two diagonals for the second. The `turns` option sets it. Half-turn boards are not turned, since a half turn looks the same however the board is turned. The code is in `packages/shared/src/duel/`.

The board is split into **orbits**: a tile and its twin (a tile on the line or point of symmetry is alone). Every tile in an orbit gets the same type, so each player sees the same surroundings, mirrored or turned. The two players start on twin cities.

- **Size:** `tiles` is about how many tiles the board has (`DUEL_TILES`: 36, 100 and 160 at small, normal and large; about 18, 50 and 80 tiles for each of the two players). There is no radius.
- **Blobs:** one starting blob (and its twin across the board) and a few extra blobs. An extra blob is either on the line (mirror) or at the middle (half turn), where it has no twin, or off it, where it comes with a twin. A half-turn board has at most one blob at the middle (decided first, 40% of boards, with the two starts put far enough out to leave it room), and none anywhere else on its point. Bigger boards have both more blobs (about one extra for every 2.2 tiles of board radius) and slightly bigger ones (radius 1.2 plus a tenth of the board's radius, at most 2.4).
- **Corridors** join the blobs. They are made for one half of the board, picked at random (a half has the blobs on its side and any on the line or point of symmetry), and then mirrored onto the other half (a mirror turns a corridor's bend the other way), so the whole layout maps onto itself. On the half, a spanning tree makes everything reachable (with the lengths shaken up a little, so the nearest blob is not always the one that is joined) and a few more short corridors make loops. Two blobs on the line of a mirror board are only joined to each other (by a corridor that runs dead straight down the middle) on 30% of boards; on the others they join the board through blobs off the line. They are narrow: 1, 2 or 3 hexes across, mostly 1. Tails and peninsulas can be any length, since the board is fair by symmetry.
- **The two starts are never joined by a corridor of their own.** They are joined only through other blobs, though the land may happen to run together. The one exception is a board with no other blobs, which only small boards have (about 60% of them): there the starts have no other way to be joined, so they get a straight corridor between them.
- **Crossovers** are corridors from one half to the other. If no blob is on the line or point of symmetry, one has to cross over to join the halves. A blob on the line or point does the job instead, as long as it is joined to a blob on one side (its twin corridor joins the other): on a half-turn board with a middle blob, that is usually the only link. Then a random number more cross over (none 40%, one 30%, two 20%, three 10%), each with its twin, as long as there are blobs on both sides left to join.
- **Drawing:** the blueprint is drawn onto hexes with noise (the same as battle royales: see below). Noise is not symmetric, so the land is what was drawn together with its image, then tidied with rules that look at the board as it was before each step, so the result stays exactly symmetric. Only the piece with the starts is kept. The city and the ring of land round it in each start blob are never removed.
- **Area:** blob sizes and counts come from the board's size, so only the distance between blobs changes as the layout is stretched to the right area. The land is then grown or shrunk by a fraction of a tile everywhere until the tile count is within 3% of the target (boards more than 8% off are thrown away and drawn again; the first try nearly always passes).
- **No fallback:** if no board can be made in 200 attempts, generation throws (it did not happen in testing), and the room tries another seed.

## Three-player boards

`createTrioMatch` makes a fair board for three players, in `packages/shared/src/duel/` next to the duel code, which it shares most of its drawing with. It is **exactly symmetric under turns of 120 and 240 degrees** about the middle of the board, so each player sees the same board from their side. There is no mirror version: a mirror cannot treat three players alike.

- **Size:** `tiles` is about how many tiles the board has (`TRIO_TILES`: 48, 120 and 210 at small, normal and large), which is 16, 40 and 70 tiles for each player: a little less than in a duel (18, 50 and 80), which has fewer players on its board.
- **Blobs:** one starting blob with two twins, a third of a turn round from one another, and **sets of three** extra blobs, one in each third (one set on normal boards, two on large, as far as they fit; small boards have no room for a set, so they have the three starts and perhaps the middle blob). A blob can also sit at the middle (on 40% of boards, decided first, with the starts put far enough out to leave it room); it has no twins. Blob sizes, the gap between blobs and the corridor widths are the same as for duels.
- **Corridors** are made for one third of the board and turned twice onto the others, the way a duel's are made for one half and mirrored. One blob from each set (and the middle blob) are joined by a spanning tree and a few loops, with the lengths shaken up a little; the rest is copies.
- **Crossovers** are corridors from a blob in one third to a twin of a blob in the next. If there is no middle blob, one has to cross over to join the thirds; a middle blob that has a corridor to a blob in one third has one to each of the others, so it can be the only link. After that a random number more cross over (none 40%, one 30%, two 20%, three 10%).
- **The starts are never joined by a corridor of their own**, unless they are the only blobs (small boards without a middle blob, which are then a triangle of three corridors).
- **No tail rule:** as with duels, tails and peninsulas can be any length.
- **Drawing, area and symmetry** are exactly as for duels: the drawn land together with its two images, tidied by rules that look at the whole board before each step, then the layout is stretched and the land grown or shrunk a little until the tile count is within 3% of the target. Boards are not turned afterwards.
- **Terrain:** a tile and its two twins are an orbit and always get the same type, so extra cities and villages come in threes and the starting cities are twins.

## Four-player boards

`createQuadMatch` makes a fair board for four players, in `duel/` beside the others (`quad.ts` lays out the blobs). It is **exactly symmetric under two mirrors at right angles**, and so under the half turn they make together. On a hex grid two mirror lines at right angles are always one of each kind: one through tile corners (up and down, as a duel's usual line) and one through the middles of tile edges (across). After the board is made it is **turned by a random multiple of 60 degrees**, so the lines can lie any of the three ways two such lines can (turning by 180 degrees changes nothing: `turns` is 0 to 2). Every tile has three twins, one in each of the other quarters, of the same type, and the four players start on twin cities.

- **Size:** `tiles` is about how many tiles the board has (`QUAD_TILES`: 60, 140 and 240 at small, normal and large), which is 15, 35 and 60 tiles for each player.
- **Blobs:** one starting blob in each quarter (the first, then its image across the vertical line, across the horizontal line, and through the middle). Extra blobs come in sets: four when off the lines (one in each quarter), two when on one of the two lines, and one when at the middle. One set on normal boards and, as far as they fit, two on large. A blob at the middle is decided first (40% of boards), with the starts put far enough out to leave it room. Small boards have no room for an extra set, so they have the four starts and perhaps the middle blob.
- **Corridors** are made for one quarter of the board (with the blobs on the lines at its edges) and copied into the other three: a spanning tree and a few loops, with the lengths shaken up a little, then **crossovers**. A corridor that is its own mirror image runs dead straight, so a corridor between two blobs on one line is allowed on only 30% of boards, as with duels.
- **Crossovers** are corridors from a blob in this quarter to an image of a blob in another. If the quarters are not already joined (through blobs on the lines, or the middle one), enough have to cross over to join them; after that a random number more do (none 40%, one 30%, two 20%, three 10%).
- **The four starts are never joined by a corridor of their own**, unless they are the only blobs (small boards without a middle blob), where they have to be joined to one another.
- **Narrow corridors, blobs well apart, no tail rule, drawing, area and symmetry** are as for duels and three-player boards.
- **Terrain:** a tile and its three twins are an orbit and always get the same type, so extra cities and villages come in fours (or twos, for tiles on a line) and the starting cities are twins.

## Six-player boards

`createSextetMatch` makes a fair board for six players, in `duel/` beside the others. Quad and sextet boards are laid out by the generic group builder in `duel/group.ts`: a `FairGroup` lists the group's symmetries, a fundamental domain, and where starts and extra blobs may sit. A hex board has two groups of six symmetries, and both are made, picked by the seed (or by the `symmetry` option):

- **Rotational (`rotational`):** turns of 60 degrees, six in all (the middle is the only tile that is its own twin). A blob at the middle has no twins; every other blob has five.
- **Mirror (`mirror`):** turns of 120 degrees together with three mirror lines 60 degrees apart (the group of a triangle, D3). The three lines run either through tile corners (`axis: 'points'`) or through the middles of tile edges (`axis: 'edges'`), picked by the seed. Blobs off the lines come in sixes, blobs on a line in threes, and a blob at the middle alone. A corridor that is its own mirror image runs dead straight, so a corridor between two blobs on one line is allowed on only 30% of boards.

- **Size:** `tiles` is about how many tiles the board has (`SEXTET_TILES`: 90, 180 and 300 at small, normal and large), which is 15, 30 and 50 tiles for each player.
- **Blobs, corridors, crossovers:** as for four players: a start blob and its images, sets of extra blobs (one on normal boards, two on large, as far as they fit), a blob at the middle on 40% of boards, corridors made for one sixth (or twelfth, for a mirror board's domain) and copied by every symmetry, then a random number of crossovers. The six starts are never joined by a corridor of their own unless they are the only blobs (small boards without other blobs).
- **No final turn:** the board is not turned afterwards; the line of a mirror board lies one way for each kind.
- **Terrain:** a tile and its twins are an orbit and always get the same type, so the starting cities are twins and extra cities and villages come in sixes (fewer on a line).
- **Previewer:** `/?maps&mode=six` (the Sextet mode), with a symmetry bar and, for mirror boards, a mirror line bar.

## Tile types

1. **Starting cities** as above (on duel boards the two twin cities; on battle royale boards one in each spawn chamber).
2. **Other cities** are rare: about one per 50 tiles, not counting the starting cities. Orbits are tried in random order, and one is added only if it brings the number of extra cities closer to that target. Cities are never closer than **3 tiles** to any other city, starting cities included, and are never placed on the edge of the board.
3. **Villages** are placed randomly wherever they fit. Each legal spot gets one with the village chance (40% by default); at 100% the board is filled until nowhere legal is left. A village never touches another village or a city. Edge tiles are only considered once no interior tile can take a village. Placing a village only removes options, so a single random pass over the orbits leaves a board where no further village fits.
4. **Farmland** is everything else.

Neutral tiles start with their type's base garrison.

## Battle Royale boards

A battle royale board has no symmetry. It is drawn from a **blueprint** by the code in `packages/shared/src/royale/`, not by `randomShape` (which only duels use).

1. **Blueprint** (`archetypes/`): an archetype lays out round **chambers** and **corridors** between them, in tile units (one unit is the distance between neighboring hex centers), never touching hexes. There is one **spawn chamber** per player, all the same size so no player starts with a roomier bowl, spread out by picking the best of several random spots for each. Neutral chambers (round rooms with no city) are supported by the layout code but the `scattered` archetype asks for none: the starting bowls fill the land, so such rooms only fit on a few boards, and look like a start with nobody in it. Corridors get a random width (1, 2, 3 or 4 hexes; about 85% are narrow enough to count as chokes) and a slight bend. Every setting is the same whatever the map size or the number of players (the only things that differ are the tiles per player, below): the same bowl size, the same corridor width weights, the same share of extra loop passages (0.3 chambers), the same spacing between start bowls. Chamber and corridor sizes are absolute: only the distances between chambers scale with the board, so a 1-wide choke is 1 wide on every map size. Only one archetype exists so far, `scattered` (chambers at random, joined by a spanning tree plus a few extra loops).

   **Topology rules** (`topology.ts`): no **tail or peninsula longer than one corridor**. A dead-end chamber may hang straight off a junction, but a dead end reached through a chain of chambers that each have only two corridors (a snake, or a dead end behind a dead end) is not allowed. The archetype fixes a tail by joining its end to the nearest chamber it is not yet joined to, which closes it into a loop, and the validator rejects any layout that still has one. Loops themselves are not required.

   **Peninsulas** are dead-end chambers: one corridor, off a junction (the rule above keeps them from being any longer). Nothing adds them on purpose. They come up where the spanning tree has a leaf, and a player's starting chamber can be one. Not every board has one (with 6 or more players, about 55% to 85% do, usually one or two; boards for 3 players rarely do). Loops come from the extra corridors beyond the spanning tree and from the tails that are closed up; boards typically have 2 to 5.

2. **Rasterize** (`rasterize.ts`): every hex center is tested against a field that is positive inside the chambers and corridors (blended smoothly where they meet) and bent by seeded noise, so rooms are lumpy and the coast is natural. The hexes along every corridor's centerline, and the city and the ring around it in every spawn chamber, are land whatever the noise says.
3. **Area:** the same blueprint is redrawn at a corrected scale (up to 6 times) until the land is within 6% of the target. A candidate more than 12% off is thrown away.
4. **Cleanup:** one-tile bays are filled, one-tile spurs trimmed, and everything but the biggest connected piece removed. The board is then recentered on the origin.
5. **Analyze** (`analyze.ts`): the finished board is measured, not the blueprint. For each corridor, the land is measured at right angles to its centerline along its middle stretch; the narrowest cross section is its width (a **choke** if at most 3 hexes) and it is a **cut** if closing it splits the board in two pieces.
6. **Validate:** a candidate must have every city on a non-edge tile (so a full ring of neighbors), cities at least `minStartDistance` apart, at least 2 chokes, no more than 40% of its land deeper than 4 tiles inland (that would be a blob). Otherwise the next seed is tried, up to 80 times in total. The reasons candidates are thrown away are in the report that `createFreeForAllMatch` returns, which the map previewer shows.

- **Size:** the target is the number of players times `tilesPerPlayer`, which is at least `minTilesPerPlayer` (20). The map size only sets `tilesPerPlayer`: 15, 25 or 45 at 6 players on small, normal and large, times `(6 / players) ^ 0.25` for other player counts (`royaleTilesPerPlayer`). Everything else is the same for every board: cities are at least 4 tiles apart (`minStartDistance`), and the spawn chambers have radius 1.6. The floor exists because the bowls are a fixed size: small boards with many players would otherwise have too little land per player to fit them (8 or more players could not be generated at all), so they get 20 per player instead.
- **Starting cities:** one per player, at the center of its spawn chamber, never on the edge. Players are assigned to them at random. There are no cities except the starting ones, and villages are placed as on duel boards.

### Villages

Villages use the same placement as duel boards (see Tile types), with each tile its own group: a random pass over the interior tiles, then the edge tiles, each place getting a village with the village chance (40%) if it does not touch a city or another village. Nothing makes the villages even between players.

The map previewer (`/?maps`) draws each board with the game's own board drawing (`Board` in the client), with a panel of generation settings beside it. The panel shows the numbers for each board, can draw the blueprint over it, and collects statistics over 100 seeds. The address can set where it starts: `/?maps&mode=ffa&size=large&players=8&seed=123&archetype=scattered&blueprint`, which also gets back to a particular board. For duels it also takes `symmetry`, `axis`, `tiles` and `turns`; `mode=trio` and `mode=quad` show the three- and four-player boards (with `tiles`; `turns` for the four-player boards).

## Settings

Every number above is a generation setting (`GenerationParams` in `packages/shared/src/params.ts`, with the defaults in `DEFAULT_GENERATION_PARAMS`): city density and spacing, village chance and spacing, the battle royale tile and spacing settings, and the coast settings that both kinds of board share. Matches use the defaults, except that the map size picks the number of tiles of a duel board and the battle royale tiles per player and city spacing (which also depend on the number of players). The generators take a `params` option with overrides, which tests use.
